Three useful distinctions

The tool is more than the model it uses.

These products are AI-powered tools, not AI models. What makes them feel and work differently is the harness that operates the AI, how the experience adapts to you, and what behavior you can deliberately configure.

01

The harness operates the AI

It is the environment that runs and controls the AI, determining what information and actions are available and what controls apply.

02

Personalization adapts the experience

It adapts to you. For example, it remembers preferences, uses your work context, or learns repository conventions.

03

Customization shapes behavior

You deliberately change how the tool behaves through instructions, knowledge sources, skills, agents, connectors, or workflows.

Each AI tool provides a different harness around its AI model or models. The harness may supply context, instructions, memory, tools, permissions, triggers, runtime, approvals, and guardrails. Which elements are available, inherited, or configurable varies by tool.

Plain-English definitions

Terms you will see in the comparison.

You do not need to memorize these. They explain where a tool gets information and what it can do with it.

Information

Context
The information available for the current task, such as your open file, meeting, email, or conversation.
Microsoft Graph
The secure connection to Microsoft 365 data and services, such as mail, calendars, people, Teams, and files.
Work IQ
Microsoft's intelligence layer that uses your permitted work information and relationships to make Microsoft 365 Copilot experiences relevant to you.

Behavior

Instructions and knowledge
Directions for how the AI should behave plus trusted information it can refer to.
Memory
Information retained from earlier work, such as preferences or project conventions.
Skills
Reusable instructions and resources packaged for a specific kind of task.

Actions and connections

Tools and actions
Capabilities the AI can use, such as searching, editing a file, sending an email, or calling a business system.
Plugins
Add-ons that connect a tool to another service or capability.
MCP
Model Context Protocol, a common way for AI tools to connect to outside data and actions. Think of it as a standard adapter.

Control

Permissions and guardrails
Rules controlling what the AI can see and do, including identity, access limits, approvals, and safety policies.
