Chapter 5 · Jobs are tasks, not titles

Bette Graham didn't try to become a better typist.

When electric typewriters made her worse at her job, she didn't optimize the wrong thing. She redefined the goal: not typing, but producing perfect documents. That reframe became Liquid Paper — and a $200M exit. AI is your electric typewriter moment.

"Don't only ask 'how do I do this faster?' Ask 'what outcome is this task really serving?'"

You're ready

Now move the work.

The audit you're about to run is Bette's reframe applied to your actual week. List your real tasks. See where AI can take work off your plate. Reinvest that time in the capabilities no machine can replicate.

You'll do this by putting your work into three buckets:

  • Bucket 1 · Automate Work AI can do alone: repeatable, predictable, rules-based, and usually with a clear right answer.
  • Bucket 2 · Assist The biggest opportunity: AI handles basics, drafts, patterns, summaries, or options; you decide what matters and make it meaningful.
  • Bucket 3 · Human The durable human zone: trust, ambiguity, ethical judgment, creative leaps, and emotional intelligence. Curate it actively as the boundaries move.
Think of the work as a conveyor belt.

Over time, more routine Bucket 1 work will disappear as AI improves. Add human judgment to the right work and new Bucket 2 opportunities emerge. Use AI well in Bucket 2 and you create time, ideas, and capacity for deeper Bucket 3 work.

Not every task must travel through all three buckets. The skill is actively curating the mix: offload work that should disappear, amplify work where AI and judgment combine, protect uniquely human work, then return about every four weeks as the boundaries move.
